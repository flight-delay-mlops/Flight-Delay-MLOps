Project – End-to-End MLOps Pipeline for Flight Delay Severity Prediction
This project aims to provide students with practical experience in designing, implementing,
and managing a complete Machine Learning Operations (MLOps) pipeline. Through a
realistic problem, students will apply best practices in automation, reproducibility,
deployment, and monitoring to deliver a fully functional predictive system.
The assignment emphasizes the engineering principles such as versioning, testing,
continuous integration, and production monitoring that ensure reliability and scalability in
real-world machine learning environments.

1. Project Overview
The project involves building an end-to-end MLOps workflow to predict the severity of
flight arrival delays using historical data from the US Bureau of Transportation Statistics
(BTS).
Each team will develop and maintain its own repository containing the complete workflow,
while a centralized server will act as the deployment and evaluation environment,
simulating a client production system.
The prediction task consists of assigning each flight to one of three arrival delay severity
categories:
Category

Definition

On time

Arrival delay below 15 minutes

Moderate delay

Arrival delay of at least 15 but less than 60 minutes

Severe delay

Arrival delay of at least 60 minutes

To mirror real-world operational challenges, data will be released in sequential rounds
representing future time periods. These rounds will simulate changes in flight
characteristics and data distributions. Evaluation will also include controlled scenarios
designed to assess inference robustness and the ability to detect performance degradation
affecting specific airlines or airports.
Ground-truth labels will become available after predictions have been generated, allowing
students to implement and demonstrate monitoring under delayed-label conditions.

2. Dataset Description
The dataset consists of historical domestic flight records from the US Bureau of
Transportation Statistics (BTS), Airline On-Time Performance Data.
Dataset: https://www.transtats.bts.gov/DL_SelectFields.aspx?gnoyr_VQ=FGJ
Each record represents an individual flight and contains a range of attributes related to
flight identification, airline and airport information, scheduled and actual flight operations,
and delay outcomes.
The predictive target is the arrival delay severity category, derived from the actual arrival
delay.
Students must use flight records from 2022 and 2023 to develop, train, validate, and
evaluate their models. They are responsible for exploring the dataset, identifying relevant
features, and implementing appropriate preprocessing and feature engineering. Only
information available before the scheduled departure of a flight may be used for prediction.
Flight records from subsequent years will be reserved for centralized evaluation of the
deployed models. Only flights that were neither cancelled nor diverted and have a valid
recorded arrival delay will be considered for model development and centralized
evaluation.

4. Technologies and Architecture
Students may use any programming language of their choice, although Python is strongly
recommended due to its extensive ecosystem for data science and MLOps development.
Code versioning must be managed through GitHub, with GitHub Actions used to implement
CI/CD workflows. Teams are expected to use MLflow (or equivalent) for experiment
tracking and model versioning, while Prometheus and Grafana are recommended for
system monitoring and performance visualization.
In addition to their local development environments, each team will require (at least) two
virtual machines (VMs): one dedicated to hosting the MLflow (or equivalent) server (and
optionally supporting DVC for data versioning) and another serving as the deployment
environment, hosting the production model and monitoring tools.
VMs can be requested through the DEI Helpdesk ( https://my.dei.uc.pt/login ). Teams are
expected to adopt computationally efficient approaches and models, simulating the
need to minimize resource requirements.
The computational resources provided by GitHub Actions' free hosted runners may be
insufficient for all training and deployment workflows. Therefore, teams are encouraged to
configure self-hosted GitHub Actions runners on their VMs, particularly since these
machines operate within DEI's network and will not be directly accessible from outside.

7. Evaluation and Grading Matrix
Students must work in groups of four.
The checkpoint will take place during a regular class, with each group presenting its
progress. All group members must participate, answer questions, and demonstrate their
individual contributions. Students who fail to demonstrate meaningful work or contribution
will receive a grade of zero. The checkpoint accounts for 5% of the final grade.
The final submission accounts for the remaining 95%. Project defenses will be conducted
individually, and every student must demonstrate a thorough understanding of the entire
project, including its architecture, implementation, and technical decisions, regardless of
who implemented each component. AI tools may support development, but all technical
decisions must be justified through sound reasoning and understanding. Referring to an
LLM's recommendations/implementations is not an acceptable justification.
Assessment will focus on the adequacy of the model development process and the
completeness and quality of the MLOps implementation. Particular emphasis will be placed
on reproducibility, automation, reliability, and engineering quality, rather than
predictive performance alone.
Projects that merely reproduce the provided baseline pipeline without demonstrating
substantial independent implementation and application of MLOps concepts may fail to
meet the minimum assessment requirements. Students who fail to demonstrate
sufficient understanding of the project and underlying concepts, including work
performed by teammates or generated using AI, or who have not made a significant
contribution to the project, will receive a grade of zero.
Continuous Training (CT) and Data Versioning (DVC) are optional components that may
contribute to bonus credit.
